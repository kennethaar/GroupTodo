One place for everything · and for everybody you are waiting on

GroupToDo, for the person in the middle

Two jobs, one tool. It catches everything you need to remember, from anywhere, in one place. And it lets you run the people side of all of it - every group, every organisation, every person who owes you something - without a single one of them having to use it.

Which is the part other tools cannot do. Most of the people you depend on will never adopt a to-do app. Most of them do not think they need one. They are right, and it does not matter: you send them an email, they tap what is done, and your list updates itself.

What it is for

Everything in, and everybody handled

One capture tool for the lot, and one place that knows who owes you what.

You are in the middle of more than one thing at once: the day job, a tender, a board you sit on, the house, the club. Each one has its own cast of people, half of whom are in other organisations entirely. The work of being you is not doing the tasks - it is remembering what everybody is supposed to be doing and finding out whether they have.

So you write everything in one place, and the tool handles the people. A thought at the kitchen table, a promise from a meeting, a thing you need from the architect - all into the same list, in whatever words you had at the time. Put somebody's name on a line and it leaves your list and lands in Waiting for: no longer something you do, something you are owed.

Then, when you want to know where everything stands, the tool asks all of them for you. That takes about two minutes, and it is the next section.

The thing nothing else does

Two minutes to hear back from everybody

A dozen open things owed by nine people across five organisations, and not one of them uses this app. Waiting for already knew who owed what. What it could not do was ask.

One file does the round trip. The other person needs no account, no login, no app and no explanation.

You used to write that mail by hand, paste four task titles into it, and type the answers back in one at a time - per person, per fortnight. Now the tool writes it, and what comes back goes in by itself. Per person it is about twenty seconds, which is why the whole round takes a couple of minutes.

  1. Ask - People, pick the person, Ask … for an update Everything open with their name on it is already listed and already ticked. Untick anything you would rather not raise, then Make the sheet. People is the whole roster of who owes you what, so you work down it: “3 things owing · asked 11d ago” tells you who is worth asking again and who you only chased on Tuesday.
  2. Send - one file, and a covering note in your own voice You get a self-contained page in your downloads folder and a note to send it with, in English or Norwegian, ready for Open in email, Send in Teams or Copy. It names the actual things you are waiting on, because that is what tells the person the mail is really from you. You attach the file yourself: a mail link is not allowed to attach anything, which is a browser rule and not a missing feature.
  3. They tick - one tap per thing they finished They double-click the attachment and it opens in whatever browser they have. A note where a note is worth typing, then one button. Nothing to install, nothing to join, nothing to remember next time - which is why it works on people who would ignore any invitation you sent them. Their ticks are kept in their own browser, so closing it halfway through costs them nothing, and the note says outright that they can ignore the attachment and just reply in words.
  4. Import - Paste a reply, or Import a file Answers usually arrive as the body of an email, so pasting is the ordinary route. Either way you get a review listing exactly what will change, every row droppable, before anything is applied. Applying closes what they ticked and files their notes as comments in their name, because they said them.
The sheet is not a copy of your list.

It carries the lines you chose for that one person and nothing else: no projects, no other tasks, no other people, no files. The architect forwarding it to his office leaks four sentences, not everything you are working on - which is the reason you can send one to anybody without thinking about it first.

Sending one stamps the task with who asked and when, and the stamp is dropped the moment they answer, so the tool keeps the score instead of you. There is a mockup of the whole exchange in docs/check-in-sheet-mockup.html - open it in a browser and tap the rows.

Many groups, one of you

A system that grows with you

You are not administering one group. You are administering several, each with a different cast, and most of those people will never be inside the tool. Both halves are normal here, and you move people between them one at a time.

One area per circle
Work, the tender, the house, the board you sit on. Each is its own area, switched between in one click, with its own name and colour. The people in one cannot see another, because it is somewhere else entirely.
Most of them stay outside, and that is the normal shape
An area with three people in it and nine being emailed check-in sheets is not a half-finished rollout - it is how this is meant to look. Nobody is upgraded against their will.
When somebody asks how you are doing it, you hand them an area
Not an invitation to a product. the areas menu at the top right → Invite somebody makes one small file that arrives with the list already set up, and their setup is the same two minutes yours was.
Once they are in, the asking stops being needed
Hand a task to somebody who is in with you and it becomes their todo while staying your waiting-for - one line read from two ends, not a copy either of you keeps up to date. When they tick it, it is done for both of you, and no sheet goes out.
Nothing to undo if a circle goes quiet
No seats, no licence, nobody to cancel with, and no accounts belonging to people who have moved on. You stop opening it, and what happened is still legible years later.
Do this now

Two minutes: who, where, what

On a computer, in Edge or Chrome. There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for, so this is the whole of it.

  1. Who - type your first and last name It is the first thing it asks. It stamps your own writing, which costs nothing; more to the point it is the name that goes on every check-in note you send out, so it is worth typing properly.
  2. Where - Choose folder, and pick one Say yes when the browser asks to let the page edit it. Put it in OneDrive, Dropbox or wherever your files already live and it is backed up like everything else. A private folder is the sensible default; sharing one is how you bring somebody in later. Skip the step and everything stays in this browser until you decide.
  3. What - Start a new list and type your first task The box is already waiting. Press Enter. Then put other people's names on things as you write them - that is all the preparation the check-in sheets need.

Your first list is a short tour: one project whose every step links straight to the part of the app it is about. Press a link and you are there. They are ordinary tasks, so reword them, reorder them or delete the lot.

Learn it by using it

A few habits, and nothing else to read

Nothing is hidden, but nothing shows up before it is about something. Options appear as you start needing them, so the screen stays about as complicated as your list is.

Today is where things land
Your day page takes whatever turns up, in whatever order, in whatever words you had. Tidying it into projects is something you do later, or never.
A task is yours by default
Write it, it is yours. Put a name on it and it is theirs. No assigning screen, no ceremony, no second list to keep in step.
Open a task and you see where it sits
The project it belongs to, the line above it, what is beside it and what is under it, laid out around it. Every box is a way in, so you can follow a plan without going back to a list. Press Esc and the arrow keys walk it.
Talk about it where it is
Every task has its own thread, so what somebody told you about a thing sits on the thing - including the notes that come back on a check-in sheet - instead of in a chat window where neither of you finds it again in March.
Tick the states you want to see
To do, Doing and Waiting for are checkboxes, so you can read them together or one at a time. A state you have never used is never mentioned.
Nothing nags
No badges, no streaks, no mail going out behind your back. It tells you what changed when you open it and is otherwise silent.
The bonus, once you care

Why there was nothing to sign up for

It has no server, so there is nothing to be a member of, nothing to pay for and nothing to be locked into.

Your tasks are plain text files in a folder you already have. That is the reason the setup above was two minutes and the reason bringing somebody in is a shared folder rather than a purchase order: the sharing is your own, not ours. Nobody buys a seat, nobody asks an administrator, and when somebody leaves you take them off the folder.

It also means the list outlives the tool. When a project is over the folder is still a readable record of it, in Notepad, with no account to close and nothing to export. And it is free, which is pleasant, but it is not the reason to use it.

Everything in a shared folder is shared. That is the only rule.

There are no per-task permissions, because the folder is the permission. If something in an area you have shared should not be seen, open it and press Alt+S (Move to another area) to put it in one of your own, because moving it is the only thing that actually makes it private.

Advanced

When you want more

Put it on your phone needs a web link, about ten minutes

Capturing from anywhere means the phone, and a phone can run GroupToDo - but not from a file. Phones refuse to give a page opened as a file anywhere to save, and they cannot open folders at all. So the phone needs a web address, and it keeps its own copy that you carry back and forth as a single file.

Do not tap the app file in your cloud app

It opens, looks fine, saves nothing, and loses what you typed when you close it. GroupToDo will warn you. Use the link.

  1. The files go on a web address Once. Your intranet, a SharePoint-hosted site, or your own always-on machine below. A document library will not work - that is file storage, not a web host.
  2. Open that address on the phone, then add it to your home screen That one is your browser's own menu, not ours: Add to Home Screen on an iPhone, Install app on Android. You get an icon, and after the first visit it opens with no signal.
  3. On the computer: Link vault file, saved beside the folder Call it work-vault.md. Next to the folder, not inside it. The computer then keeps it up to date on its own.
  4. On the phone: Open vault file to collect, Send vault file to post back Two taps, one each way. Nothing is ever overwritten - both sides' work survives.
Everything in sync, no file passing one always-on machine

If you have a machine that is always on - a spare PC, a Pi, a NAS - it can serve the list to your own computer and phone, and to anybody you have shared an area with, live both ways, with no vault file to carry. It needs a password and ten minutes of setup. README.md has the commands.

Other ways to look at the same list board, calendar, timeline, report, map

Under Views: a board you drag cards across, a month calendar, a timeline whose bars you drag to reschedule, a report of what was finished over the last month or quarter, and a map of how everything links together.

None of them appears before your files have something for it to show. An empty calendar is not a feature, so nothing with no dates in it has a calendar.

What waits on what when one person's answer holds up three projects

On a project page, nothing comes first opens a list of your other projects: tick the ones that have to finish first. The project then says blocked by Site survey while anything in it is open, the timeline draws the chain, and moving one project offers to push what is downstream along. The order lives in the file, so it travels with the folder.

Dates in your calendar Outlook, Google, or a file

Anything with a date gets an Add to calendar button, and Planned can send the lot at once. Two ways out, and the difference is worth knowing: a downloaded file is made on your own machine and goes nowhere, while the Outlook and Google links open their new-event screen with the title and date carried in the web address.

Nothing is ever subscribed. A live calendar feed would mean your whole list sitting at a web address guarded by nothing but the fact that nobody has guessed it, so there is deliberately no such thing here.

If it is not behaving the things that actually go wrong
I sent a check-in and heard nothing
Nothing in the app chases it, by design - it sends no mail of its own. People shows asked 11d ago so you can see who has gone quiet, and sending another sheet is the same three clicks.
They replied in words and ignored the attachment
Expected, and handled: Paste a reply takes the body of the email and shows you what it proposes to change before anything happens.
A warning that nothing can be saved
You opened it inside something that will not let it store anything - usually a preview in a document library. Download the file and open it from your computer.
“browser only - no files” at the bottom of the sidebar
No folder chosen yet, so nothing is being written. Click that line, then Choose folder. On a phone this is normal and expected.
You cannot find the files
Only a computer writes them, and only after you pick a folder. Look inside it: journals\ is one file per person per day, pages\ one file per project and list.
Nothing leaves your machine on its own. The app is blocked from reaching the internet at all, which is also why there is nothing to log in to - a check-in sheet is a file you attach yourself, to a mail you send yourself.
