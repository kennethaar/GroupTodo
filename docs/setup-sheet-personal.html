Two minutes, on a computer

Setting up GroupTodo for yourself

A to-do list that keeps everything as ordinary text files in a folder you already have. Nothing to install, no account, no password, and nobody else involved. Two minutes and you have your own list.

Doing something with other people? There is a companion sheet, Setting up GroupTodo for a group. You do not need it to start.

What this is

Almost everything you write down is yours alone. So that is what this is.

You write a task, you do it, you tick it off. No assigning, no inbox, no permissions, no team to set up, and nothing on screen implying a colleague who is not there. A list with one person in it looks like a list for one person.

What it does have is the other half of your list: the things other people owe you. The supplier, the architect, your manager, your brother-in-law. None of them is going to adopt your to-do app, so the app asks them for you, with one file and no sign-up at their end. That is Getting an answer out of somebody, below.

And the list outlives the tool. The files open in Notepad.

Do this now

Who, where, what

  1. Who - type your first and last name It is the first thing the app asks. On your own it mostly stamps your own writing, which costs nothing; it is also the name that goes on the check-in notes you send out, so it is worth typing properly.
  2. Where - Choose folder, and pick one Say yes when the browser asks to let the page edit it. From here on your tasks are files in that folder, saved as you type - put it in OneDrive, Dropbox or wherever your files already live and it is backed up like everything else. Skip it and everything stays in this browser until you decide.
  3. What - Start a new list and type your first task The box is already waiting. Press Enter. Use Edge or Chrome.

That is the whole of it. Any folder will do; a private one is the sensible default, and nothing about this stops you sharing a different folder later.

Your first list is a short tour: one project whose every step links straight to the part of the app it is about. Press a link and you are there. They are ordinary tasks, so reword them, reorder them or delete the lot.

Learn it by using it

A few habits, and nothing else to read

Nothing is hidden, but nothing shows up before it is about something. Options appear as you start needing them, so the screen stays about as complicated as your list is.

Today is where things land
Your day page takes whatever turns up, in whatever order. Tidying it into projects is something you do later, or never.
A task is yours by default
Write it, it is yours. No assigning, no ceremony, no second screen to keep in step.
Open a task and you see where it sits
The project it belongs to, the line above it, what is beside it and what is under it, laid out around it. Every box is a way in, so you can follow a plan without going back to a list. Press Esc and the arrow keys walk it.
Tick the states you want to see
To do, Doing and Waiting for are checkboxes, so you can read them together or one at a time. A state you have never used is never mentioned.
Waiting for is not a to-do list
Put somebody's name on a thing and it leaves your list and lands in Waiting for, which is the honest place for it: it is no longer something you do, it is something you are owed.
Nothing nags
No badges, no streaks, no mail. The app tells you what changed when you open it and is otherwise silent.
The part that is worth the setup

Getting an answer out of somebody

Everything you are waiting on is owed by somebody, and most of those people are never going to use this app. Waiting for already knew what they owed you. What it could not do was ask.

One file does the round trip, and the other person needs no account, no login and no app.

You used to write the same mail by hand every fortnight, paste four task titles into it, and type the answers back in one at a time. Now the app writes it, and what comes back goes in by itself.

  1. Ask - People, pick the person, Ask … for an update Everything open with their name on it is already listed and already ticked. Untick anything you would rather not raise, then Make the sheet.
  2. Send - one file, and a covering note in your own voice You get a self-contained page in your downloads folder and a note to send it with, in English or Norwegian, ready for Open in email, Send in Teams or Copy. It names the actual things you are waiting on, because that is what tells the person the mail is really from you. You attach the file yourself: a mail link is not allowed to attach anything, which is a browser rule and not a missing feature.
  3. They tick - one tap per thing they finished They double-click it and it opens in whatever browser they have. A note where a note is worth typing, then one button. Their ticks are kept in their own browser, so closing it halfway through costs them nothing - and the note says outright that they can ignore the attachment and just reply in words.
  4. Import - Paste a reply, or Import a file Answers usually arrive as the body of an email, so pasting is the ordinary route. Either way you get a review listing exactly what will change, every row droppable, before anything is applied. Applying closes what they ticked and files their notes as comments in their name, because they said them.
The sheet is not a copy of your list.

It carries the lines you chose and nothing else: no projects, no other tasks, no other people, no files. Somebody forwarding it leaks four sentences, not your vault.

Sending one stamps the task with who asked and when, so People can tell you "3 things owing · asked 11d ago" rather than making you remember, and the stamp is dropped the moment they answer. There is a mockup of the whole exchange in docs/check-in-sheet-mockup.html - open it in a browser and tap the rows.

Advanced

When you want more

Put it on your phone needs a web link, about ten minutes

A phone can run GroupTodo, but not from a file. Phones refuse to give a page opened as a file anywhere to save, and they cannot open folders at all. So the phone needs a web address, and it keeps its own copy that you carry back and forth as a single file.

Do not tap the app file in your cloud app

It opens, looks fine, saves nothing, and loses what you typed when you close it. GroupTodo will warn you. Use the link.

  1. The files go on a web address Once. Your intranet, a SharePoint-hosted site, or your own always-on machine below. A document library will not work - that is file storage, not a web host.
  2. Open that address on the phone, then add it to your home screen That one is your browser's own menu, not ours: Add to Home Screen on an iPhone, Install app on Android. You get an icon, and after the first visit it opens with no signal.
  3. On the computer: Link vault file, saved beside the folder Call it work-vault.md. Next to the folder, not inside it. The computer then keeps it up to date on its own.
  4. On the phone: Open vault file to collect, Send vault file to post back Two taps, one each way. Nothing is ever overwritten - both sides' work survives.
Everything in sync, no file passing one always-on machine

If you have a machine that is always on - a spare PC, a Pi, a NAS - it can serve the list to your own computer and phone, live both ways, with no vault file to carry. It needs a password and ten minutes of setup. README.md has the commands.

Other ways to look at the same list board, calendar, timeline, report, map

Under Views: a board you drag cards across, a month calendar, a timeline whose bars you drag to reschedule, a report of what was finished over the last month or quarter, and a map of how everything links together.

None of them appears before your files have something for it to show. An empty calendar is not a feature, so nothing with no dates in it has a calendar.

Dates in your calendar Outlook, Google, or a file

Anything with a date gets an Add to calendar button, and Planned can send the lot at once. Two ways out, and the difference is worth knowing: a downloaded file is made on your own machine and goes nowhere, while the Outlook and Google links open their new-event screen with the title and date carried in the web address.

Nothing is ever subscribed. A live calendar feed would mean your whole list sitting at a web address guarded by nothing but the fact that nobody has guessed it, so there is deliberately no such thing here.

Separate parts of your life work, home, volunteering

Each one is its own folder, called a space. Work, Home, Volunteering: separate lists, switched between in one click, each with its own name and colour. Two spaces can never share a folder and one can never sit inside another, so if you ever do share one of them, what you shared is exactly what you meant to share.

If it is not behaving the three things that actually go wrong
I sent a check-in and heard nothing
Nothing in the app chases it, by design. People shows asked 11d ago so you can see who has gone quiet, and sending another sheet is the same three clicks.
A warning that nothing can be saved
You opened it inside something that will not let it store anything - usually a preview in a document library. Download the file and open it from your computer.
“browser only - no files” at the top
No folder chosen yet, so nothing is being written. Vault & sync → Choose folder. On a phone this is normal and expected.
You cannot find the files
Only a computer writes them, and only after you pick a folder. Look inside it: journals\ is one file per person per day, pages\ one file per project and list.
Your tasks stay in your folder. The app cannot send them anywhere - it is blocked from reaching the internet at all, which is also why there is nothing to log in to.
