GroupTodo · mockup

Waiting for updates

You are waiting on four things from Mia in Finance. Mia does not use GroupTodo and is not going to start today. So you send her one file, she ticks what she has done, and her answers come back into your list.

No account, no invite, no server - the same bargain as the rest of GroupTodo. The file is the round trip.

The exchange

Four steps, two of them yours

  1. Ask · you People → Mia Halvorsen → Ask for an update. The four things you are waiting on are already listed and already ticked to include. Press Send.
  2. Send · you GroupTodo writes check-in-mia-halvorsen.html - one self-contained file. Email it, Teams it, drop it in a share. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
  3. Tick · Mia She double-clicks the file. It opens in whatever browser she has. She taps the two she finished, types one note, presses Send back - her email opens with the answers already in it, and she presses Send. Nothing to attach.
  4. Import · you Her answers land in your inbox as a few plain lines. Paste them into GroupTodo and you get this review: two tasks to close, one comment to file. Press Apply.

Step 2 · what you send

The covering note

Language Sets the language of the note and of the sheet she opens, together.

Mia is about to get an HTML attachment from somebody. That is exactly what a phishing mail looks like, so the note it arrives with has to do the one thing a phishing mail never does: name the actual work, in your voice, and tell her she is free to ignore the attachment entirely.

New message - to mia.halvorsen@example.com
Tomia.halvorsen@example.com
Subject
Attach

      

        Open in email
        Send in Teams
        
      

      

    

  


  
Why each line is there. The four items are named, because a
  stranger could not have known them - that is what tells Mia this is really from you. The
  file is described in plain words, not as "the attached form". And the last line gives her
  a way out that costs you almost nothing: if she would rather just reply in words, the
  answers still arrive, and you type them in. A request nobody is allowed to refuse is a
  request people learn to ignore.


  
You attach it yourself. The same
  mailto: limit applies going out, so the button fills in the address, the
  subject and the whole note, and you drag check-in-mia-halvorsen.html in before
  you press Send - one drag, once. In Teams there is nothing to attach at all: the message
  goes in the chat and you drop the file in the same window.

      Step 3b · the way back
      
  
      No attachment, on purpose
      
  
      A mailto: link carries to, subject and
  body and nothing else - a page that could pre-attach a file off your disk
  could mail your disk to a stranger. So Mia's reply travels as the body of the email,
  which needs nothing of her but pressing Send. The sheet measures the link as she ticks;
  past the two thousand characters Outlook truncates at, the button changes to
  Save the reply and attach it rather than quietly sending half an answer.
